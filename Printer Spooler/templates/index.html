Theoretical Connection:

In Dijkstra’s Dining Philosophers problem, competing threads (philosophers) require exclusive access to shared resources (forks). In our Printer Spooler, multiple user threads submit print requests to a single printer. Instead of risking circular wait and deadlock, our spooler mediates access through a centralized FIFO queue and protects printer operations via a strict Mutex Critical Section.

Operating System Synchronization & Deadlock Guide (Viva Notes)

Core theoretical concepts implemented in this mini-project
1

What is a Printer Spooler?

SPOOL stands for Simultaneous Peripheral Operations On-Line. Since physical printers operate much slower than CPUs and application threads, jobs are spooled into a buffer/queue. This prevents client applications from blocking while waiting for sheets of paper to print.

2

The Need for Synchronization

When multiple users or client threads dispatch print jobs simultaneously, uncoordinated access causes race conditions. Without synchronization, pages from different users would interleave, corrupt counters, and crash memory. Synchronization enforces orderly, predictable sharing.

3

Critical Section & Mutual Exclusion

The code segment that operates on the shared hardware resource (the physical printer) is the Critical Section. We enforce Mutual Exclusion using a binary semaphore / mutex (threading.Lock). Exactly one job can hold the printer lock at any instant.

4

FIFO Scheduling & Starvation Freedom

Jobs are placed into a thread-safe FIFO queue (queue.Queue). Jobs are processed strictly in their submission order. This guarantees fairness and ensures no user process suffers from indefinite postponement (starvation).

5

The 4 Necessary Deadlock Conditions

According to the Coffman conditions, a deadlock can occur if and only if all 4 conditions hold simultaneously:

  • Mutual Exclusion: At least one resource is held in a non-shareable mode.
  • Hold and Wait: A process holds a resource while requesting additional resources.
  • No Preemption: Resources cannot be forcibly taken from a process holding them.
  • Circular Wait: A closed chain of processes exists where each holds a resource needed by the next.
6

How Our System Prevents Deadlock

Our design breaks the Circular Wait and Hold and Wait conditions. Client threads never hold one printer while requesting another; they simply drop a request into a single FIFO queue and terminate their request phase. The worker acquires only a single mutex at a time and releases it before picking the next job.
